**Gist:** The derivative of velocity with respect to time — equivalently, the second derivative of position.

$$a_t = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{V_{t + \Delta t} - V_t}{\Delta t} \qquad a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2 x}{dt^2}$$

**Why it matters:** The same limiting move as [[Instantaneous velocity|instantaneous velocity]], applied one level up. Position, velocity and acceleration form a single chain of derivatives, which is why [[Kinematic equations|constant acceleration]] makes position a quadratic in $t$.
