**Gist:** When acceleration is constant, position is a quadratic in time — and the three coefficients turn out to be the initial position, the initial velocity, and half the acceleration.

$$x = c_1 + c_2 t + c_3 t^2 \qquad v = c_2 + 2 c_3 t \qquad a = 2 c_3$$

$$c_1 = x_0 \qquad c_2 = v_0 \qquad c_3 = \tfrac{1}{2} a$$

**Why it matters:** Differentiate twice and the constants fall out with physical meaning attached. Worked in the lecture as $x = 8 - 6t + t^2$, giving $v = -6 + 2t$ and $a = 2$ — noted there as the shape of a single segment in motion planning.
